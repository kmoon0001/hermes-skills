# Short-Seller / Activist Backgrounding: Reference Guide

Use when a short-seller report or activist investor targets a skilled nursing facility operator. This guide structures how to investigate the accuser in parallel with the claims, producing a balanced assessment.

## The Entity Behind "Hunter" Accusations Against Ensign (June 2026)

The accuser is **not** an individual named "Hunter" — it is **Hunterbrook Media / Hunterbrook Capital LP**, a hybrid investigative news outlet and hedge fund that profits from short-selling the companies it investigates. The name is a portmanteau of co-founder Sam Koppelman's middle name "Hunter" (after Hunter S. Thompson) and co-founder Nathaniel Horwitz's mother's surname (author Geraldine Brooks).

### Key Identity Facts
- **Founders**: Sam Koppelman (publisher, age 28, Harvard Crimson alum, political speechwriter, son of "Billions" showrunner) + Nathaniel Horwitz (CEO, age 28, Harvard Crimson, biotech investment background, mother Pulitzer winner Geraldine Brooks)
- **Funding**: Seed from Emerson Collective (Laurene Powell Jobs), Marc Lasry (Avenue Capital), David Fialkow (General Catalyst), Peter Kolchinsky (RA Capital). Later: Ford Foundation at $100M valuation
- **Fund performance**: +23% net first 9mo 2025 (per WSJ); +16% H1 2025 (per FT)
- **Advisory board**: Paul Steiger (ProPublica founder), Bethany McLean, Daniel Okrent, Matt Murray — legitimate journalism figures providing credibility
- **Business model**: Newsroom investigations shared with hedge fund before publication → fund trades (usually shorts). Also partners with litigation firms via "Hunterbrook Law."
- **GC**: Fitzann Reid — former SEC lawyer, formerly at activist hedge fund Engine No. 1

### Previous Targets & Outcomes
- United Wholesale Mortgage (UWM): stock fell, then recovered; Ohio AG sued; company calls it "sham"
- Winnebago Industries: class action filed; company called claims "baseless"
- Dexcom: stock fell 10% on allegations about CGM accuracy; outcome pending
- RadNet: dropped a key metric after Hunterbrook exposed it
- Hit rate: < 50% on trades turning into market-moving events (per Business Insider)

### Key Credibility Flags
- **Disclosed short position** on ENSG — stands to profit from stock decline
- **Anonymous sources** — many claims from unnamed former employees with grievances; no independent verification
- **One-sided presentation** — Ensign declined to comment; no company response included
- **Andrew Left parallel** — published 7 days after Left (Citron Research) was convicted of securities fraud for essentially the same model
- **Extrapolation from worst case** — methodology starts with Riverbend (a "Special Focus Facility" — CMS's worst designation)
- **Old settlements repackaged** — $48M False Claims Act settlement from 2013 presented as fresh
- **Limited market impact** — ENSG fell ~5%; JPMorgan (Overweight) and UBS (Neutral) maintained ratings after management calls

## Backgrounding Playbook

When a short-seller attack lands, investigate the **accuser** in parallel with the **claims**.

### Phase 1: Who Is the Accuser?
- Entity structure — media outlet, hedge fund, or hybrid? Who profits and how?
- Founders and key personnel — age, experience, affiliations, conflicts
- Funding sources — activist investors, political donors, competitors behind them?
- Track record — which previous targets? Outcomes? Hit rate?
- Business model — do they profit from negative coverage? Are positions disclosed?
- Relationship to target — any disclosed short/long position?

### Phase 2: Map the Claims
- Specific allegations vs. rhetorical framing
- Data-driven (CMS, MedPAC, public records) vs. anecdotal (anonymous former employees)
- Genuinely new vs. repackaged from past settlements/lawsuits
- Methodology transparent and reproducible? What assumptions embedded?
- Company's response (or silence) accounted for?

### Phase 3: Triangulate with Counter-Data
- **Quality metrics**: CMS 5-star ratings from `cms-nursing-home-data`
- **Staffing**: CMS Payroll-Based Journal (PBJ), MedPAC staffing reports, state-level minimums
- **Industry benchmarks**: MedPAC median turnover (53% for SNFs, 2023), AHCA/NCAL data
- **Analyst coverage**: post-accusation rating changes by JPM, UBS, RBC
- **Company data**: earnings call transcripts, ESG reports, investor presentations
- **Trade press**: McKnight's LTC News, Skilled Nursing News, Modern Healthcare

### Phase 4: Assess Credibility
- Structural conflicts of interest
- Anonymous vs. named sources
- Inflammatory language vs. measured analysis
- Track record accuracy on past targets
- Regulatory/legal action against the accuser (e.g., Andrew Left conviction)
- Timing relative to market events

## Common Short-Seller Narratives Against SNF Operators
1. Understaffing-to-profit model — high-acuity patients, low staffing
2. Related-party tunneling — affiliates for rent/management/insurance
3. Gaming CMS star ratings — self-reported metrics inflated
4. Acquire-and-cut staffing — reduce nurse hours after acquisition
5. Whistleblower accounts — disgruntled former employees
6. Political influence — donations to anti-regulation campaigns

## Key Counter-Narrative Patterns
- **Industry context**: staffing shortages & turnover affect ALL SNFs (MedPAC: 53% median turnover)
- **Star ratings**: if target has 85% 4-5★ and 4.4 avg (vs 3.7 national), systemic neglect is contradicted
- **Old settlements**: prior settlements (e.g., 2013 $48M FCA) ≠ current wrongdoing
- **No regulatory action**: absence of CMS/DOJ action post-report undercuts claims
- **Analyst retention**: maintained ratings after management calls = market unconvinced
